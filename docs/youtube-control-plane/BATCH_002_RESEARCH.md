# Batch 002 — Research Record

Twenty episodes, selected from a candidate pool of roughly sixty.

This file is the evidence trail. Every factual claim in a Batch 002 narration should be
traceable to a row here. **No citation appears in spoken narration** — sources live here so
that a later script revision, metadata pass, or visual QA check can find out *why* a claim was
made without re-researching it.

---

## Method

1. **Generate broadly.** ~60 candidates across the families in the channel brief — hidden
   safety features, manufacturing artefacts, tiny holes, code requirements, leftovers that
   survived into modern products.
2. **Gate on facts first.** Score is irrelevant if the payoff cannot be verified. Rejections
   at this step are listed below and are not scored.
3. **Verify what survives** against manufacturers, regulators, standards bodies, patents and
   institutions. Secondary sources were used for discovery; authoritative ones for the claim
   that carries the episode.
4. **Optimise the batch, not the topic.** The final twenty were chosen for spread across
   object, environment, mechanism type and title grammar — not by taking the top twenty
   scores.

### The myth guard did real work

This niche is full of confidently repeated internet folklore. The rule applied throughout:
**if the evidence supports a current function but not an original intent, write the function.**
Three candidates were cut specifically on this, and one survived only because its historical
intent is genuinely documented (the revolving door's 1888 patent).

## Rejected — factual gate

| Candidate | Why it was cut |
|---|---|
| **Bread tag colours = day of week** | No industry-wide standard exists. The colour-to-weekday mapping is real *inside* individual bakeries and varies between them, so the payoff ("blue means Monday") cannot be stated as generally true. The printed date is the only binding indicator. Fails the myth guard outright. |
| **Wine bottle punt** | Every explanation offered (pressure, sediment, stacking, pouring grip, glassblowing leftover) is plausible and none is decisively supported as *the* reason. A topic whose payoff is "nobody agrees" is not a Bill Finds Out episode. |
| **Pasta spoon hole = one portion** | Widely repeated, poorly supported, and hole sizes are not standardised across manufacturers. |
| **Shirt buttons on opposite sides by gender** | The common explanations are folklore with no reliable primary support. |
| **Soda tab rotates to hold a straw** | The tab does rotate and a straw does fit. It was not designed for that, and a title implying design intent would be false. |

## Selected — the twenty

Score is editorial, out of 100: familiarity 20 · curiosity gap 20 · payoff 20 ·
visualizability 15 · fact confidence 10 · hookability 5 · short-form fit 5 · batch novelty 5.

### 1. `tape-measure-hook` — 92
**That Wobbly Tape Measure Hook Is Not Broken**
The loose hook everyone assumes is worn out is a compensator.
**Verified:** the hook slides by exactly its own thickness (~1/16"), so inside and outside
measurements both read from true zero. Named explicitly in US Patent 4466194, *"True zero tape
hook"*. **Sources:** US Patent 4466194; US Tape; Family Handyman.

### 2. `windshield-frit-dots` — 90
**What The Black Dots Around Your Windshield Are Doing**
The black border and its fading dot pattern are two different jobs.
**Verified:** the frit is ceramic enamel fired into the glass; it shields the urethane adhesive
from UV and improves bonding. The dot matrix creates a thermal gradient, because black glass
heats faster than clear and an abrupt boundary concentrates stress.
**Sources:** Motor1; NuVision Auto Glass; Rick's Auto Repair; enamel composition patents US8202812 / US8007930.

### 3. `coin-reeded-edges` — 89
**Your Quarter Is Still Protecting Silver It Doesn't Have**
Ridges defeat a fraud that stopped being possible.
**Verified:** reeding was introduced against coin clipping, when coins carried their value in
metal. Isaac Newton used it as Warden of the Royal Mint from 1696. Retained today for
counterfeit resistance, machine sorting and tactile identification.
**Sources:** US Gold Bureau; Mental Floss; APMEX. *Historical intent is documented here — this is one of the few episodes allowed to claim it.*

### 4. `foil-shiny-dull` — 88
**The Shiny Side Of Foil Is A Manufacturing Accident**
Two sides, no difference, and a real reason.
**Verified:** at the final rolling pass foil is too thin to survive alone, so two sheets are
milled together. Outer faces take the polished rollers' mirror finish; inner faces do not.
Reflectivity differs by ~2%, far too little to affect cooking.
**Sources:** ScienceABC; Reynolds guidance via Reader's Digest; Family Handyman.

### 5. `tactile-paving` — 94
**The Bumpy Tiles On The Sidewalk Are A Two-Word Language**
A two-symbol vocabulary readable through a shoe.
**Verified:** domes = warning/stop, bars = directional guidance. Invented by Seiichi Miyake in
Okayama, Japan, 1965, self-funded, for a friend losing his sight. Adopted on all Japan Railway
platforms by 1975; required under the ADA in the US.
**Sources:** Victoria & Albert Museum collection record; CNN; TIME.

### 6. `airplane-ashtray` — 91
**Planes Still Have Ashtrays And It's Not An Oversight**
A fixture for a banned activity, required by law.
**Verified:** the FAA lists the lavatory ashtray as minimum equipment; a broken one is a
dispatch defect. The reasoning is documented — without a disposal point, a cigarette ends up in
the paper waste bin. A 1973 Varig lavatory fire killed 123 people.
**Sources:** FAA position quoted in Marketplace; Forbes; Simple Flying. *EASA has since relaxed this for new type designs — the script says "regulators", not "every regulator forever".*

### 7. `third-brake-light` — 86
**Why Cars Got A Third Brake Light**
A light that exists because the other two are ambiguous.
**Verified:** CHMSL, mandated on US passenger cars from 1986, light trucks from 1994. Early
fleet trials suggested ~35% fewer rear-end collisions; long-run crash data settled nearer
**4.3%**. *Both numbers are in the script.* Reporting only the first would be the dishonest
version of this episode.
**Sources:** NHTSA crash-data analysis; APA; Detroit News 30-year review.

### 8. `sneaker-lace-lock` — 87
**Sneakers Come With An Anti-Blister Setting**
The unused eyelet is a feature nobody switched on.
**Verified:** the extra eyelet enables a heel lock / runner's loop, which clamps the collar and
stops heel lift — the friction that causes rear-heel blisters.
**Sources:** Gear Patrol; Advnture; Lock Laces technique guide.

### 9. `padlock-drain-hole` — 82
**The Hole In A Padlock Lets Water Out, Not In**
A hole in a lock that protects the lock.
**Verified:** primary role is drainage — trapped moisture rusts or freezes the pin stack.
Secondary role is a lubrication port.
**Sources:** Monroe Engineering; manufacturer maintenance guidance.

### 10. `ferrite-choke` — 85
**Charger Cables Have A Lump That Stops Them Broadcasting**
A cable is an antenna, and this is the mute button.
**Verified:** a ferrite bead/choke is a lossy inductor that impedes high-frequency common-mode
current and dissipates it as heat, needed for electromagnetic emissions compliance.
**Sources:** Electrical Technology; HowStuffWorks; Fair-Rite component data.

### 11. `brick-holes` — 84
**A Brick Has Holes For Three Different Reasons**
Holes make a solid object better at being solid.
**Verified:** mortar keys through the perforations locking courses together; reduced solid
thickness lets clay dry and fire evenly without cracking; less clay, less kiln fuel, lighter to
handle. *Note: "frog" is strictly the indentation; through-holes are perforations or cores —
the script says "holes" and avoids the term.*
**Sources:** Modular Clay Products; Stonehenge Masonry; Eng-Tips practitioner discussion.

### 12. `railway-ballast` — 85
**That Gravel Under Train Tracks Isn't Landscaping**
Four jobs done by loose stones.
**Verified:** distributes concentrated sleeper loads onto the subgrade; angular stones
interlock to resist lateral track movement; drains water away from the bed; damps vibration.
**Sources:** Wikipedia *Track ballast* with cited engineering references; railway permanent-way guidance.

### 13. `chip-bag-nitrogen` — 88
**Chip Bags Are Not Filled With Air**
The thing you resent is doing two jobs.
**Verified:** bags are nitrogen-flushed to below ~3% oxygen, because oxygen turns frying oil
rancid; the gas volume ("slack fill") also cushions fragile product in transit.
**Sources:** PureAire Monitoring Systems; Pneumatech; packaging-industry references.

### 14. `toilet-seat-gap` — 86
**Public Toilet Seats Have That Gap Because Of A Rulebook**
An inconsistency between home and public fixtures, explained by a code.
**Verified:** open-front seats for public use appear in the American Standard National Plumbing
Code (1955) and the Uniform Plumbing Code (IAPMO) since 1973. *These are codes adopted by
jurisdictions, not federal law — the script says "the plumbing codes most of the country builds
to".*
**Sources:** HowStuffWorks citing ASNPC/UPC; Mental Floss; IAPMO UPC.

### 15. `soda-can-neck` — 89
**Why Soda Cans Get Narrower At The Top**
A taper worth a hundred thousand tons.
**Verified:** can ends are far thicker than walls, so shrinking end diameter saves
disproportionate metal. End diameter fell ~6 mm (60→54) since the 1960s, saving on the order
of 90 million kg (~200 million lb) of aluminium a year.
**Sources:** MUNDOLATAS packaging industry analysis; Christian Science Monitor; beverage-can engineering breakdowns.

### 16. `revolving-door` — 90
**A Revolving Door Is Solving A Problem You Can't See**
A worse door that solves an invisible problem.
**Verified:** patented 1888 by Theophilus Van Kannel, Philadelphia, explicitly to counter air
infiltration. Counters stack effect — buoyancy-driven pressure difference in tall buildings.
Always sealed; only the compartment's air transfers. MIT study measured large HVAC savings.
**Sources:** US patent record and Van Kannel history; Energy Vanguard; Boon Edam technical course; MIT study via Hackaday.

### 17. `convex-mirror-warning` — 87
**Why One Of Your Car Mirrors Comes With A Warning**
A legally required admission that a mirror is lying.
**Verified:** FMVSS 111 requires the US driver-side mirror to be flat unit-magnification;
the passenger side may be convex, and every convex mirror must carry the warning text
permanently marked. Amended 1982. *US-specific — the script opens "On an American car".*
**Sources:** NHTSA FMVSS 111 interpretation letters; ScienceABC.

### 18. `beer-bottle-brown-glass` — 91
**Light Turns Beer Into Something Close To Skunk Spray**
Real photochemistry with a memorable product.
**Verified:** riboflavin acts as photosensitiser; 350–500 nm light degrades hop-derived
isohumulones into a thiol chemically close to skunk spray. Brown glass blocks ~98% of the
relevant wavelengths; green far less; clear none.
**Sources:** CulinaryLore chemistry breakdown; The Cary Company; light-stability patents US5811144 / US5582857.

### 19. `cabin-lights-dim` — 88
**Why The Cabin Lights Go Down Before Landing**
A comfort gesture that is actually evacuation preparation.
**Verified:** dark adaptation takes several minutes; takeoff and landing carry the highest
evacuation likelihood. Dimming pre-adapts vision and makes floor path lighting and exit signage
read clearly.
**Sources:** Monroe Aerospace; Simple Flying; AFAR aviation-expert explainer.

### 20. `thermal-receipt-fade` — 88
**Your Receipt Was Never Printed With Ink**
A print process that never finishes.
**Verified:** thermal paper carries a colourless leuco dye and a developer in a surface
coating; the print head applies heat, they melt together and react. The reaction does not lock,
so heat, UV, friction and some plastics keep driving it.
**Sources:** Jota Machinery thermal-paper technical notes; shop4rolls; YG Group.

**Mechanism types** — deliberately spread so no two episodes resolve the same way:
manufacturing artefact · material economics · photochemistry · surface chemistry · gas
packaging · optics · human dark adaptation · human signal ambiguity · regulatory code ·
mechanical compensation · friction · drainage · electromagnetic filtering · load distribution ·
anti-fraud · accessibility encoding · building pressure.

**Script statistics:** 20 scripts · avg 92 words · range 87–96 · 9,931 narration characters
total. Batch 001 ran 86–99 words for comparison, so pacing is consistent with published work.

**Openings** are varied by construction: imperatives (*Look along…*, *Run your thumb…*,
*Flip a padlock…*), existence (*There is a hard lump…*), adverbial (*Somewhere in the back
window…*), and plain declaratives. No opening is a rhetorical question.

**Titles** avoid a single template: four begin *Why*, three *The*, and the rest use distinct
constructions. No title claims *secret*, *dangerous*, *illegal* or *life-saving*.
